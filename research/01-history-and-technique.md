# 1982 Reiki Convention Music: how it was made

*Research notes, Nov 1 2026. Explore pass #1.*

Target sound: private-press new age cassettes, roughly 1975–1990. Halpern, Iasos, Slepian, Demby, Stearns, Laraaji, Emerald Web, David & Steve Gordon, plus the long tail on [Sounds of the Dawn](https://www.youtube.com/@soundsofthedawn). **Not** drone, **not** "nothing happens" space ambient, **not** vaporwave.

How sure each claim is: **[primary]** means the artist's own words. **[credits]** means liner notes or label credits. **[secondary]** means a review or article. **[inference]** means my own synthesis, and it is labeled as such.

---

## 0. The one idea that unlocks everything

The music changes all the time, but the changes don't happen in the harmony.

In Western tonal music, movement comes from tension and release: dominant → tonic, leading tone → root, a beat setting up the downbeat. Halpern's move was to take that engine out. The "evolving" feeling you hear comes from everything else:

- **timbre** (filters opening, phasers sweeping, a flute fading into a vocoder choir)
- **texture and density** (voices added and dropped)
- **register** (a line drifting up an octave)
- **space** (echo tails piling up, reverb blooming)
- **slow pitch-collection shifts** (the key moves the way a cloud moves, without a cadence)

Learn to make change along those axes and you have most of the genre. Section 4 is the toolkit for that.

## 1. Halpern's own theory: "anti-frantic" music, music without tension

The best primary source I found is Halpern's introduction to *The New Age Music Guide*, titled ["Notes on New Age Music"](https://newagemusic.guide/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Notes-on-New-Age-Music-by-Steven-Halpern.pdf) **[primary]**. He sets out the genre against the five standard musicology axes:

| Axis | What Halpern says |
|---|---|
| **Harmony** | Consonance rather than dissonance. That "doesn't mean just using syrupy major chords," but tense jazz/rock voicings "such as a C7#9 or a C7b5 are conspicuously absent." "Dissonant harmonies, which strain rather than uplift, are consciously avoided." |
| **Melody** | When you hear do-re-mi-fa-so-la-ti, you know the final *do* is coming. "It's precisely this melodic inevitability that keeps us locked into predetermined states." Classic pieces (Paul Horn's *Inside*, his *Spectrum Suite*, Iasos's *Inter-Dimensional Music*) are ones where "most people cannot remember the sequence of sounds." **No hooks.** He elsewhere calls the conditioned wait for resolution "Scalus Interruptus Syndrome" (from *Tuning the Human Instrument*, 1977, and *Sound Health*, 1985). |
| **Rhythm** | "Perhaps the most striking aspect of New Age music is its use of rhythm, or, more accurately, its lack of it." "New Age music is not based on a pulse." He grants the Riley/Reich/Schulze pulse subgenre, but the core move is "doing away with the pulse entirely," so listeners "no longer have the same perceptual and contextual cues to help them anticipate the next beat." Also: "If it features a big beat, yes, you can dance to it, but no, it isn't New Age music." |
| **Timbre** | Avoid harsh attacks and shrill overtones (violins, trumpets, metal guitar, "synthesized percussion"). Instead use **"electric piano, harp, flute, bells, and 'eq'd' string ensembles."** |
| **Texture / space** | "Space" via reverb and echo "is not simply a 'special effect,' but rather an integral part of the music itself." He points to caves, ziggurats and cathedrals. Technology now gives "unlimited sustain at the flick of a switch (of the electronic reverb and echo device)." |

Two more of his points:
- Sustain is what makes it possible. A harpsichord can only make short-note music, and a breath-limited trumpet can't hold a note forever. Rhodes plus echo, synths and reverb make a music of tied whole notes possible.
- On the craft of background music: "it is actually quite tricky to create music interesting enough to withstand attentive listening yet not conflict with or distract from the primary activity." This is the same needle you're trying to thread.

### Spectrum Suite (1975/76), the template
- Recorded at **The Music Annex, Menlo Park, CA, 1975**, on solo **Fender Rhodes**, with Iasos on **electric flute** ([Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/2979642-Steven-Halpern-Spectrum-Suite), [MVD reissue notes](https://customers.mvdb2b.com/s/StevenHalpernSpectrumSuite45thAnniversaryCollectorsEdition/IPM8777)) **[credits]**. First issued as *Christening for Listening* ([Ultravillage](https://ultravillage.com/steven-halpern)).
- **Seven tracks, seven keys, ascending:** C (red, root chakra), D, E, F, G, A, B (violet, crown) **[credits]**. Each piece hovers in one key center. The album "moves" between pieces by stepping up the scale, never by cadencing inside a piece.
- A review calls them "major-key improvisations that never resolve" ([Cue Records](https://www.cue-records.com/A-Z-Artists/H/Halpern--Steven-Halpern/-8148.html?language=en)) **[secondary]**. AllMusic: "melodies not tied to a discernable rhythm pattern" ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/spectrum-suite-mw0000197106)).
- MVD's description of the sound: "a series of tuning forks or crystal bowls played by a keyboard." The Rhodes's bell-like tine attack and long, pure decay are why it was his instrument.
- Later Halpern added **Prophet-600 + E-mu Emulator** (*Shared Visions*, 1986, w/ David Friesen) and **Lyricon** wind synth (*Natural Light*, w/ Dallas Smith) ([organicmusic.jp](https://organicmusic.jp/en/products/steven-halpern-david-friesen-shared-visions)) **[secondary]**.
- **Rings of Saturn (1981):** I found **no instrumentation credits anywhere**. One source says it was cassette-only and among his "more abstract and even haunting" work ([musicofourdesire](https://events.musicofourdesire.com/artist/steven-halpern)). This is a real gap. The inlay or Halpern's archive would be the place to look.

**Scientific honesty note:** Halpern's physiological claims (alpha/theta induction, 7.83 Hz Schumann resonance, Hans Jenny cymatics, John Diamond kinesiology) are his framing, not settled science. A 1984 study (Logan & Roberts) found listeners to Halpern's music had *higher* tension than a no-music group on two of nine measures. The compositional principles are still a great design brief, whatever the mechanism.

## 2. The artists: gear and technique, sourced

### Iasos (1947–2024): "paradise music"
Primary sources: [RBMA 2011](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2011/08/interview-new-age-pioneer-iasos-in-words/), [Psychedelic Baby 2020](https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2020/04/iasos-interview.html).

- **The core trick: volume pedal + Echoplex swells.** "I was running sounds through compressors, into a volume pedal, into an Echoplex, and that was going to a speaker for feedback." He'd **pluck a string with the volume off**, then raise the pedal so the note "swells in and echoes," and cut it again before the next note **[primary]**. Every note loses its attack and gets a tape-echo tail. This is the bowed-angel sound.
- **Signal chains [primary]:**
  - flute → **phase shifter → Echoplex → volume pedal**
  - slide guitar → **wah → sustainer → Echoplex → volume pedal**
- **Recording:** a quarter-track 7.5 ips **stereo** deck plus a quarter-track 7.5 ips **4-channel** deck in a small home studio. That's a ping-pong overdub workflow **[primary]**.
- **Synths:** he first found them "very cold, sterile, inorganic." Then:
  - **RMI Keyboard Computer.** You define a waveform by punching in 32 numbers that draw the first quarter of a cycle, which gets mirrored into a full wave. That's additive, hand-drawn timbre **[primary]**.
  - **ARP Pro DGX ("Heart Flame").** Digitally controlled analog mono. He loved its **portamento and pressure sensitivity** and used it in concert for decades **[primary]**.
  - **Yamaha DX7 ("Sapphire")** with his own sound library **[primary]**. His MIDI rig was "the Golden Harp" ([Banbantonton](https://banbantonton.com/2024/01/14/iasos-inter-dimensional-music/)).
  - **Vocoder choirs**: "feeding his synths through a vocoder" ([Banbantonton](https://banbantonton.com/2024/01/14/iasos-inter-dimensional-music/)) **[secondary]**. *Angels of Comfort* (1978) was an **ARP String Ensemble** improvisation **[secondary]**.
- **Influences:** Respighi, **Ravel, Debussy**, Martin Denny, Hendrix ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iasos_(musician))). The impressionists are the harmonic key here (see §3).
- **Philosophy:** the musician as a "time architect" who "creates forms in time." He heard the music internally for years before he could make it. He produced everything himself so no one else's "vibrations" got in. Later work was mixed in "3-D stereo" for headphones **[primary]**.
- Listener descriptions ([iasos.com/quotes](https://iasos.com/quotes)): "feedback loops, slowed tapes, chorus-delays and infinite sustain"; "grand, sweeping arpeggios"; "spiral ascensions."

### Don Slepian: *Sea of Bliss* (1980)
- Made on the **Bell Labs Digital Synthesizer ("Alles Machine,"** designed by Hal Alles) during his 1979–81 residency. It was recorded **Feb 3–4, 1980** and is the only full-length piece ever realized on the machine, which was taken apart in 1981 ([Boomkat](https://boomkat.com/products/sea-of-bliss), [Igloo](https://igloomag.com/profiles/an-interview-with-don-slepian)).
- **Generative sequencing:** with engineer Greg Sims he ported algorithms he'd first built on **EML 400/401 analog sequencers**. They allowed **"two levels of mathematical permutations on a list of up to 150 notes using real-time controls"** **[secondary/reissue copy]**. So: a fixed, consonant pitch set that's endlessly reordered, with Slepian steering it live. That's how it twinkles forever without repeating or ever leaving the mode.
- Effects: "a very early and somewhat crude implementation of **digital reverb and digital pitch-shifting**," and he **controlled both with his voice through a microphone** **[primary, Igloo]**.
- "I added **analog processing to digital sounds to warm up cold timbres**." He also used "**controlled randomness** in both composition and sound processing" **[primary, Igloo]**.
- His aesthetic: improvised electronic music in **baroque, classical and romantic styles** **[primary]**. That's why Slepian sounds more like *music* than drone.
- *New Dawn* (his other early cassette): **Korg PS-3100, Mellotron voices & flutes, analog tape echo** ([Jahtari](https://jahtari.org/product/don-slepian-new-dawn-lp/)) **[credits]**.
- He promoted Laurie Spiegel's **Music Mouse** (see §4.8).

### Laraaji
Sources: [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/141/laraaji), plus search summaries of the Reverb gear article and MusicWorks.
- **36-string open-tuned zither** with the **chord bars removed and a pickup attached**, through pedals. In the 80s he **hammered it with chopsticks** for sustained shimmer **[secondary]**.
- The principle: it works best when "**all the strings are ringing simultaneously and there's no one melodic figure sticking out**" **[primary]**. It's a cloud of a whole scale, which only works if the scale has no clashing intervals (pentatonic or open tunings).
- What Eno did on *Day of Radiance* (1980) **[primary, as Laraaji recalls]**:
  - **Eventide H910 Harmonizer** "to accent some of the overtones"
  - **double-tracking** the zither for "that luscious chorus experience"
  - **slowing one track's tape speed**, which gave "more of a gentle movement, rather than a rushed dance"
  - layering extra parts over the first takes for a "spacious, luscious sound"
- **Boss DM-1** analog delay on his Kalimba tracks. He also set a zither on top of an **electronic shruti box at full volume** so the strings rang **sympathetically** **[secondary]**.
- "Music should offer itself to the quietude of the listener." **[primary]**

### Constance Demby: *Sunborne* (1980), *Novus Magnificat* (1986)
- *Sunborne*: her **custom hammered dulcimer** (built with Sam Rizzetta, with an **extra bass octave**), the **Space Bass** (a 10-ft bowed sheet-metal sculpture) and **Whale Sail**, plus zither, gamelan, cheng, tabla, tambura and synth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_Demby)). It was the tape that got Sounds of the Dawn's founder hooked: "a masterpiece" ([Tabs Out](https://tabsout.com/?p=12083)).
- *Novus Magnificat* credits: Demby on **E-mu Emulator II, Roland Juno-60, Yamaha C-9 grand**; Michael Stearns on **Serge modular, The Beam, DX7, Oberheim OB-8** ([library record](https://swantest.aspendiscovery.org/Record/a2170781)) **[credits]**. Sound On Sound: "almost entirely Emulator II" ([SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/constance-demby-mystery)).
- **Her pad technique** (from SOS, a friend describing her string playing): "**each voice disappearing or being added until a new chord is built**", and then it "immediately starts eroding like a sand castle." **This is the clearest description I found of how the harmony evolves without cadences.** See §4.4.

### Michael Stearns
- *Planetary Unfolding* (1981) was made **entirely on a Serge modular**, introduced to him by **Kevin Braheny**, who worked for Serge ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Unfolding), [Santa Fe New Mexican](https://www.santafenewmexican.com/pasatiempo/fresh-off-album-release-santa-fe-soundscape-artist-michael-stearns-performs-at-paradiso/article_67fb738f-77bc-492c-84b6-2f8ea54000d5.html)).
- **The Beam**: a **12-ft, 24-string** "cosmic harp" he built ([Echoes](https://echoes.org/2022/05/25/wednesday-june-8-2022-michael-stearns-interview-2/)).

### Emerald Web (Kat Epple & Bob Stohl)
- **Lyricon** (wind synth with a bass-clarinet mouthpiece) and Epple's flutes over **ARP 2600, Moog, sequencers**. They were also associated with Buchla, EML, Computone and Orchestron ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_Web), [Psychonaut Elite](https://psychonautelite.substack.com/p/review-valley-of-the-birds-by-emerald)).

### Pauline Anna Strom
- Home studio in the Tenderloin: **Tascam 4-track, Yamaha DX7, TX816, CS-10**, and per another profile a Prophet-10 and an Emulator ([Textura](https://www.textura.org/archives/s/strom_transmilleniamusic.htm), [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/longreads/pauline-anna-strom-matters-time)).

### David & Steve Gordon (Sequoia Records)
- Their 1982 debut was mostly **piano and acoustic guitar**, with some synth and **nature sounds they recorded in Sequoia National Forest** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_%26_Steve_Gordon)). I found no gear details for *Lightspring*.

### David Gibson (Sound Healing Center, SF)
- He's not an 80s-tape artist. He's a later sound-healing producer who founded the Globe Sound Healing Institute and says he has "60 CDs on the market," used in hospital integrative-health departments. "Most of the CD's in the catalog incorporate **binaural beats** to entrain the listener" ([Sound Healing Center](https://soundhealingcenter.com/therapy/staff-and-contact/)).
- He also wrote ***The Art of Mixing***, the mixing book built on **3D visual representations of where each sound sits in a mix** (left/right, front/back from volume and reverb, up/down from frequency). The 4th edition (Routledge, 2026) adds chapters on **sound healing and psychoacoustics** and **brainwave entrainment and binaural beats** ([Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/The-Art-of-Mixing-A-Visual-Guide-to-Recording-Engineering-and-Production/Gibson/p/book/9781041104759)).
- **Why he matters here:** he's the one person in this list who has written a production manual. His "sound in space" mixing model fits Halpern's point that "space" is part of the music. Binaural beats are the one technique on his albums the 80s tapes generally didn't use: a few Hz of difference between left and right ears (for example 200 Hz vs 206 Hz gives a 6 Hz "theta" beat), which needs headphones. Evidence for the entrainment effect is mixed. As a texture, it's a slow, subliminal wobble.
- **Next:** I found no instrumentation or tuning details for his albums. The 4th-edition chapters are the place to look.

### Eno, for context
- *Discreet Music* (1975): an **EMS Synthi AKS** feeding **two Revox reel-to-reels** in a long tape-delay loop, with an EQ changing the timbre ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discreet_Music)). *Music for Airports*: short phrases on **tape loops of different lengths** (one source gives 63 s and 68 s), so the combinations never repeat ([Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/deconstructing-brian-eno-music-for-airports/)).

## 3. The music theory **[mostly inference, grounded in Halpern's rules above]**

What all of this has in common: **remove the pitches that pull, keep the ones that float.**

### Why tension happens, mechanically
Tonal pull mostly comes from **half steps next to stable notes**: ti→do (leading tone), fa→mi, and the **tritone** inside a dominant 7th (in G7, B and F want to collapse onto C and E). Halpern's banned chords, C7#9 and C7b5, are dominants with even more tritone and half-step grind.

### Scales that float
1. **Major pentatonic** (C D E G A). It has **no half steps and no tritone**, so no note "needs" to go anywhere. Every note sounds fine against every other. That's why Laraaji can let 36 strings ring at once, and why it suits the "all notes ringing" Echoplex wash. It's the workhorse.
2. **Lydian** (C D E F# G A B). The raised 4th removes fa→mi, so the IV→V→I engine is gone. It sounds bright and hovering, "suspended above the ground." **I ↔ II (C ↔ D/C)** over a tonic pedal is a classic float vamp.
3. **Lydian pentatonic / hexatonic subsets** (C D E F# G B, etc.) when full Lydian gets too busy.
4. **Major/Ionian, with the leading tone handled gently**: use B as part of Cmaj7 or a passing color, never as a leading tone resolving up.
5. **Mixolydian or Dorian for the "earthier" tapes** (the Deuter, Kitaro and Aeoliah side). Minor-ish but static, because there's no V7→i.
6. Halpern's **chakra keys**, C through B ascending. If you want a period-faithful concept album, there's a ready-made structure. (Later Halpern reissues retune to A=432 Hz.)

### Chords and voicings
- **Pedal points everywhere.** Hold the root and let the upper chords drift. The bass never cadences.
- Use **add9, sus2, 6/9, maj7, maj9, maj7#11** (that last one *is* Lydian). They're rich but not tense.
- **Quartal / open-fifth voicings** (stacked 4ths and 5ths, Debussy and Ravel style). Ambiguous and open, which suits the Iasos impressionist lineage.
- **Planing**: move the same voicing shape around over the pedal. The color changes without any functional progression.
- **Instead of V→I**, use **IV/I** (an F triad over a C bass), **ii/I**, **vi↔I**, or **bVII/I** sparingly. If you want the *feeling* of an arrival, go **Vsus4 → I** and leave out the 3rd of V so the leading tone never sounds.
- **Common-tone modulation** for key changes. Pick a new key that shares most of the notes, hold the shared notes, and let the others slide in one at a time. That's Demby's "voices disappearing or being added until a new chord is built" (§4.4).

### Melody
- **Stepwise, slow, rubato.** Phrase endings should land on **stable non-tonic tones** (the 3rd, 5th, 9th or 6th), so a phrase never "arrives" at do but never hangs in tension either. That's the opposite of a hook. It's the practical answer to Halpern's "Scalus Interruptus."
- Leave **long gaps**. The echo answers the phrase, so the space between notes becomes part of the line (Halpern: "the spaces between the notes often speak as eloquently as the primary notes").
- **Glides and portamento** (Iasos's Heart Flame). Pitch bends make melody without needing intervals that pull.
- Don't repeat yourself in a way someone could hum back. Halpern's test is that people can't remember the sequence of notes.

### Rhythm
- **No grid.** Free time, or arpeggios so slow and unquantized they read as weather.
- If there's motion, make it **several independent cycles of different lengths**, each running at its own rate (Eno's tape loops, Slepian's permutations). You hear endless motion and no meter.
- Most period "arpeggiator sparkle" was either played by hand or ran on a sequencer *without* drums. A pulse with no kick drum feels very different from a beat.

## 4. Production toolkit: how to make it evolve

Period tools first, then modern equivalents. A ★ marks a technique I'm confident is central to the sound.

### 4.1 ★ Swell everything (the Iasos move)
- **Then:** a volume pedal before an Echoplex. Pluck with the volume down and swell in.
- **Now:** slow amp attack (0.5–3 s) on plucked or keys sources, or an expression CC. Put the swell *before* the delay so each echo carries the swell shape.
- Why it works: the attack is the most "tense" part of a sound, and swells remove it. Flute, guitar and Rhodes all turn into bowed light.

### 4.2 ★ Tape echo with feedback as an instrument
- **Then:** **Maestro Echoplex EP-2/EP-3** (Iasos, Slepian's "analog tape echo"), Roland RE-201 Space Echo, two-Revox loops (Eno), Boss DM-1/DM-2 bucket-brigade analog delay (Laraaji).
- **Character:** each repeat is **darker and slightly wobblier** than the last (high-frequency loss, wow and flutter). Repeats blur into a pad instead of stacking up like digital clones. Ride feedback to the edge of self-oscillation and back.
- **Now:** any tape-delay emulation. Keep feedback at 50–80%, low-pass the repeats around 3–5 kHz, add a touch of wow and flutter, and use long times (500 ms – 2 s). Gently modulating the delay time gives the pitch-warble "slowed tapes" sound.

### 4.3 ★ Big, soft space (reverb)
- **Then:** plates and chambers. **Lexicon 224 (1978)** brought long digital halls to pro studios. By 1986 the **Alesis Midiverb** (£395, 12-bit, presets only) put digital reverb into home studios ([Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/alesis-midiverb)). [inference: much of the late-80s cassette long tail likely sits on Midiverb-class grainy halls, and that low-bit, slightly metallic tail is part of the "tape" flavor.]
- **Now:** long halls (4–12 s) with darkened tails, and pre-delay of 30–80 ms so the dry note stays clear. Reverb *after* echo is the classic order. For the cassette-era home-studio sound, a Midiverb or Lexicon emulation beats a pristine modern algorithm. (Free Midiverb impulse responses exist; [CDM](https://cdm.link/free-alesis-midiverb-ir/).)

### 4.4 ★ Voice-by-voice harmony morphing (the Demby move)
- Never change all the notes of a chord at once. Fade a single voice out or in, or move one voice a step, over several seconds. After a few moves you've arrived at a new chord, with no "chord change" event and so no cadence.
- On a synth: play a held chord and add or release one finger at a time, with long attack and release envelopes. In a DAW: put each pad voice on its own track with its own slow fades.

### 4.5 Slow modulation at unrelated rates
- Filter cutoff, phaser rate, chorus, pan and brightness should each get their own very slow LFO (periods of 7 s, 11 s, 23 s, 37 s…). With unrelated periods the overall texture almost never repeats. That's the modulation version of Eno's 63 s / 68 s loops.
- **Phasers** were central: Iasos ran his flute through one. MXR Phase 90/100 or an Electro-Harmonix Small Stone at very slow rates gives the iconic "breathing" sweep. [The pedal models are my inference; Iasos only said "phase shifter."]

### 4.6 Chorus, ensemble and double-tracking
- **Then:** the **Roland Juno-60's built-in chorus** (on *Novus Magnificat*) uses two hard-panned delay lines modulated in opposite directions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Juno-60)). Also string machines like the **ARP String Ensemble/Solina**, which has its own ensemble chorus (Iasos, *Angels of Comfort*), and literal **double-tracking** (Laraaji's zither).
- **Now:** Juno-style chorus on pads, plus a second take of the same part played slightly differently, panned opposite.

### 4.7 Pitch-shift tricks
- **Eventide H910 (1975)**: Eno used it on *Day of Radiance* to "accent overtones." Slepian had crude digital pitch-shifting he controlled with his voice.
- **Now:** blend in a detuned copy (±5–15 cents) for shimmer. Putting an octave-up pitch shifter *inside* the delay or reverb feedback loop makes rising "angelic" trails. [Caution: the full "shimmer reverb" as a named effect is a later, 1990s–2000s development. A Harmonizer in a feedback loop is the period-plausible version.]
- **Half-speed tape:** record a part, then slow it down (it drops an octave and gets gentler). That's what Eno did to Laraaji's track.

### 4.8 ★ Generative-but-constrained note streams (the Slepian move)
- A fixed list of consonant pitches (say a pentatonic set over 3 octaves), **permuted** by rules you steer in real time. The notes are always new and the harmony never wanders.
- Close relatives: Laurie Spiegel's **Music Mouse** (1986), an "intelligent instrument" where mouse movement maps onto pitches locked to diatonic, pentatonic, **quartal** and other scale/chord modes ([Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Music_Mouse)). Eventide revived it as a plugin in 2026 ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/music-mouse-by-eventide)).
- **Now:** a probabilistic arpeggiator or sequencer locked to a scale, with several voices at different, non-synced step lengths, randomized velocity and no swing grid.

### 4.9 Warm up the digital
- Slepian ran digital sounds through analog processing "to warm up cold timbres." DX7 and Emulator sounds of the era were usually softened by tape, analog mixers, chorus and reverb.
- **Now:** low-pass every digital layer a bit, add gentle tape saturation, and don't let FM bells sit naked at full brightness.

### 4.10 The cassette itself is part of the sound
- [inference] Many of these releases went from 1/4" or 4-track masters to **real-time or high-speed cassette duplication** with Dolby B, and listeners now hear rips of worn tapes. Rolled-off highs, slight wow, hiss and a soft high end are a big part of the "1982 reiki convention" feel. Use a cassette emulation on the master bus, lightly: an 8–12 kHz rolloff, a little flutter and a whisper of hiss.

## 5. The instrument palette

Use it as a checklist. Each one is something sustained, bell-like or breathy.

| Role | Period sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bell-keys foundation | **Fender Rhodes** (Halpern), DX7 electric pianos and bells (after 1983) | Tine attack, long pure decay. Halpern's "tuning forks played by a keyboard." |
| Breath and lead | **Flute** (Iasos, Epple), **Lyricon** (Stohl, Halpern w/ Dallas Smith), ARP Pro DGX with portamento and pressure | Swell it, phase it, echo it. |
| Strings, harp, zither | **Harp**, **hammered dulcimer** (Demby), **open-tuned zither** (Laraaji), The Beam (Stearns), autoharp, kalimba | Let everything ring, in pentatonic or open tuning. |
| Pads and choirs | **ARP String Ensemble**, **Juno-60**, **Emulator II** choirs (Demby), **vocoder choirs** (Iasos), Prophet-5/600, OB-8, Korg PS-3100, Mellotron voices (Slepian) | "EQ'd string ensembles" in Halpern's words: dark and soft. |
| Sparkle | DX7 / TX816 bells (Strom), glockenspiel, crystal and tingsha, "twinkling sonic snowflakes" (Slepian) | High, sparse, heavy echo. |
| Earth | **Nature recordings**: forest (Gordons), water, birds (Emerald Web's *Valley of the Birds*), dolphins and whales | They give the ear a "world" without needing rhythm. |
| Deep low end | Bowed metal (Demby's Space Bass), tambura and shruti drones, organ pedal, Serge or Moog bass | Sustained, never a bassline that walks. |

## 6. A starter recipe [inference]

Something to try once we start making sounds:

1. **Key:** D Lydian or D major pentatonic. Keep a low D pedal (organ, tambura or soft synth) going the whole time.
2. **Foundation:** a Rhodes, played rubato: open 5ths, add9 voicings and slow rolled chords. Run it through gentle autopan or tremolo [Rhodes Suitcase "vibrato" is really stereo autopan; whether Halpern used it is unverified], then tape echo at around 700 ms with 60% feedback and dark repeats, then a 6 s hall.
3. **Pad:** a Juno-style saw/pulse pad, Juno chorus II, 2 s attack, 5 s release. Change chords **one voice at a time** (§4.4), so the chord moves about every 20–40 s.
4. **Air:** flute or breathy lead **swelled** in (§4.1) through a slow phaser into the same echo. Long gaps between phrases, ending on the 3rd or 9th.
5. **Sparkle:** a DX7-style bell on a scale-locked, randomized, unsynced arpeggio (§4.8). Very quiet, long echo.
6. **World:** a forest or stream bed, with an EQ notch so it doesn't fight the music.
7. **Evolution plan (a 20-minute side):** one key the whole way. Every 2–4 minutes something enters or leaves. The filter and register drift upward over the side. Around 2/3 of the way through, step up to the next key via common tones (C→D→E… is Halpern's whole-album version of this).
8. **Master:** a light cassette emulation, with no limiter pumping.
